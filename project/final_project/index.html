Understanding the application process

Applications submitted beginning in May 2027 for fall 2028 matriculation are officially part of the 2028 AMCAS application cycle.

1. Primary applications

Most MD programs use the American Medical College Application Service (AMCAS). Your primary application contains your coursework, experiences, personal statement, MCAT scores and other information.

Most DO programs use AACOMAS, which follows a separate application timeline.

Explore AMCAS →

2. Verification

AMCAS reviews your submitted coursework against your official transcripts. Verification can take several weeks, especially during busy periods.

3. Secondary applications

Medical schools may send additional applications containing school-specific essays and questions. Secondary fees and deadlines vary by school.

4. Interviews

Interviews commonly occur from late summer through the winter and spring. Schools may use traditional interviews, multiple mini interviews (MMIs), or a combination of formats.

5. Admissions decisions

Depending on the school, applicants may receive an acceptance, rejection, waitlist placement or continued review. Some schools use rolling admissions, while others release decisions in groups.

Official AAMC application timeline →

Congratulations! You got in. Now what?

Holding your seat

Follow the instructions included in your acceptance letter. Schools may require written confirmation, additional forms, or a deposit to reserve your seat.

Deposit amounts, refund policies and response deadlines vary by school. Record these dates immediately.

Plan to Enroll (PTE)

AAMC's Choose Your Medical School tool typically begins offering Plan to Enroll in February. This allows applicants to indicate their current preferred school.

Before the late-April deadline, applicants may generally continue holding other acceptances or waitlist positions, subject to school-specific policies.

April 15: Narrow your acceptances

Under standard AAMC recommendations, applicants should narrow their acceptances to no more than three schools by the April 15 milestone.

April 30: Choose one acceptance

Applicants should narrow their acceptances to one medical school. Applicants who select Plan to Enroll may generally remain on other waitlists if permitted.

Commit to Enroll (CTE)

Selecting Commit to Enroll means that you have made your final decision and must withdraw all other applications, acceptances and waitlist positions.

Schools establish their own Commit to Enroll deadlines, which may extend into the summer.

AMCAS selections do not replace communicating directly with your medical schools. Confirm the official 2028 dates and your school's policies.

What if I get waitlisted?

Waitlist movement can occur throughout the admissions cycle, particularly during the spring and summer as applicants narrow their acceptances.

  • Follow the school's waitlist instructions.
  • Check your email and applicant portal.
  • Send updates only if the school permits them.
  • Keep track of existing acceptance deadlines.
  • Be prepared to respond quickly to an offer.

There is no universal waitlist release date. Some applicants receive offers shortly before orientation.

AAMC Fee Assistance Program

The AAMC Fee Assistance Program helps eligible applicants reduce the cost of preparing for and applying to medical school.

Potential benefits

  • Reduced MCAT registration fees
  • MCAT preparation resources
  • Access to MSAR
  • An AMCAS fee waiver covering up to 20 school designations under current program terms

How to apply

  1. Create or sign into your AAMC account.
  2. Review the current eligibility and household income requirements.
  3. Complete the Fee Assistance Program application.
  4. Submit the required financial documentation.
  5. Wait for approval before paying for services covered by the program.
Apply before paying for covered services. Fee assistance benefits are generally not retroactive. Confirm the eligibility and benefits for your application year.
